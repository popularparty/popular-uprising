Chapter Two

Functional Representation

¶The Popular Party exists because America keeps misdiagnosing its own failure.

¶There’s the personnel problem, that we need better leaders. The partisan problem, that the other party is the enemy. The moral problem, that we need better values, we need to act more righteously. The policy problem, that we just need the right reforms. The messaging problem, that good ideas fail because they aren’t communicated well, or people just aren’t educated enough. These are all red herrings. What we have is a representation problem. More than that, we have an epistemic problem.

¶The Greatest Lie that the government tells about itself is that it represents the people. This lack of representation is not a failure of the system. It is not a failure in leadership alone, either. This is the system functioning as designed. Federalism distributes sovereignty geographically. In the 18th century, before the railroad and the telegraph, let alone all the technological advances of the 20th and 21st centuries, this was a defensible compromise. People were ultimately tied to the land they lived and worked on. Until the mid-20th century, communities were segregated, either totally or in part, not just by race and ethnicity, but also by denomination, class, and other fundamental differences in lifestyle. Geography as the basis of political organization was not arbitrary, it was a description of social reality. The territory was the community.

¶This is no longer the case, and it hasn’t been broadly the case for as long as many of us have been alive. Today’s representatives are tasked with the job of representing vast multitudes of different people with nothing in common but a zip code. Our elected officials are somehow supposed to represent people from all different backgrounds, different lifestyles, different perspectives, and different interests all at the same time. It’s an impossible task; even the leaders who are truly dedicated to the public good have no choice but to fail. The end result is that no one ends up being spoken for except for those with the money and connections to make their voices heard louder than all the others. Everyone else is represented only in name and abandoned in fact.

¶The alternative follows once you determine what exactly it is that representation is doing.

¶The idea of representation as symbolic embodiment of the “will of the people” was the 18th-century ideal of what representation was supposed to do, but not what representation is actually observed to be as a process. Representation properly understood is information flowing upward. The people are selecting a representative to speak and vote on their collective behalf: it is the process by which the people make their voices heard. Representation is the architecture by which the state sees the conditions it governs. Maximizing the upward flow of real and useful information is necessary for maximizing representation.

¶The federal system fails at representation the moment the district expands past the point where the people voting together no longer share real interests. Democracy isn’t preference aggregation. It is a means of falsification, the mechanism through which reality talks back to those in power and forces correction. Functional Representation is how we get back to real democracy.

¶Everyone who has ever worked within any structure at any level has executed a decision made by someone who had no idea what the fuck was going on. A directive from a boss who has never done the work. A policy that ignores every obvious real-world constraint because the policymaker has never lived within those constraints. When those who decide don’t pay, bad decisions are guaranteed.

¶Those who live within a domain carry its consequences. Their livelihood depends on knowing what’s going on. Workers of labor. Property owners of property ownership. Soldiers of war. No one governs what they do not live within: that is Functional Representation.

¶The political domain is no different. Governance is a function. It requires expertise, sustained commitment, and above all, accountability that is specifically political. The current political class is not primarily accountable to the public good. They are accountable to financial interests, factional interests, and their own personal interests. A political class whose stake is genuinely political has none of these to serve. It answers to the order it administers because the order is all it has. Those who govern must live within what they govern, and within nothing else. Today’s politicians go out of their way to stylize themselves as working class, public servants, and men of the people all while living like kings. The incentive structures of the Regime reward them for lying to you while they betray your interests and pamper themselves.

¶The political class of the successor order must be the inverse of this. A class that is truly constituted of servants. A political monastery. Their reward function cannot be contingent on holding office or what the office affords. Their reward must come from the success of the order they administer and the success of the people they govern. The incentive structure must be aligned so precisely with the public good that service to any other interest becomes self-destruction. An office that cannot enrich its holder cannot be purchased. A political class that lives within and is subject to the order it governs has no incentive to govern against it.

¶What Functional Representation uniquely solves is the informational problem. The principle is simple: a representative who is what he represents knows it better than anyone else can. The merchant who works in commerce understands commerce more deeply than any congressman who has only ever read about it. The farmer who brought in the harvest understands agricultural conditions from experience in a way no agency that has only surveyed them can. The soldier who has fought in the trenches understands war in a way no civilian appointee ever will. These representatives are not infallible, but they are working from direct contact with the reality they’re tasked with speaking for. They are harder to deceive in their own field. The information they surface has better context and fewer distortions from intermediaries. The governed and the governing are not separated by the representative structure, but continuous with each other through that very structure of representation. This is not romanticism about the good honest working man and his plight against the entitled white-collar office workers of society. Finance is a function. Banking is a function. Clerical work is a function. Functional Representation is epistemic architecture.

¶Epistemic engineering is what follows. The Regime has more data than any state in human history but it is epistemically blind. It drowns in information but cannot see. Functional Representation surfaces usable information about real functions. Territorial representation must surface real information about real territories. The political monastery ensures that the apparatus serving these functions does not corrupt the process with personal interest. The whole system needs to be designed for sight. The process must ensure that real-world data is accurately collected and processed into useful information that accurately models real-world conditions. For the people to be represented they must be seen and they must be heard.

¶Functional Representation is not so much a proposed machine as it is a governing philosophy. It’s the principle that will be used to judge all future machines. This document exists to outline the mission and worldview of the Popular Party. It exists to identify problems and solutions on the level of first principles.

¶Policy follows worldview. Policy without worldview is only administration. Any movement that begins with policy suffocates itself under the weight of administration it does not yet have the capacity to carry. Specifics will always have to change when the idea meets reality. It is better to draft a clear worldview, a clear direction that future policy can be derived from, than it is to start with policy, and try to revise it through reverse engineering to fit future needs later.

¶Theory is only as good as what it builds.

¶This is what every failed dissident movement could not produce: leaders who did the work of building the institution rather than the work of producing more theory about the institution. The dissident right has spent fifteen years debating its own positions and has produced nothing operational. The dissident left has spent even longer with the same results. They have produced more theoretical content than any prior dissident generation in American history and nothing to show for it.

¶When dissident movements are not producing more theory, they are policing each other. Cancel culture is its most visible form, but the underlying pattern is universal amongst dissident factions: ideological purity tests, expulsion of insufficiently committed allies, public denunciation as movement-building activity. None of this builds anything. They are cargo-culting institutional discipline when they lack the institutional structure that would make such discipline meaningful. It’s all debate and no praxis, which is the only thing that could actually prove any theory true or false. Every hour that is spent cancelling an insufficiently pure ally is an hour not spent building something. The movements are eating themselves through the very norm enforcement that is supposed to signal their seriousness about their cause.

¶They are acting as though correct moral commitments produce political capacity. This is exactly backwards. The moral order of a society is what its institutions produce through the reward functions they present, the discipline they impose, the culture they cultivate, and the rewards and punishments they distribute. Without the institutional foundation, moral commitments are pure abstractions. Commitment to abstract moral norms that aren’t actually present or socially enforced in real life is just complete schizo nonsense.

¶The only moral norm that any young political movement actually has the ability to have or enforce is party loyalty. Expulsion is the only mechanism the party has. When abstract ideology is the entire basis of your imaginary movement, of course it devolves into ideological purity tests. That is the entire basis of participation.

¶The Popular Party is not putting the ideological cart before the institutional horse. We design the architecture, we build the architecture, the moral norms proceed from that architecture. As a young organization, we recognize the limits of expulsion and only enforce what expulsion can presently address: loyalty to the party and its director. The member is loyal or he is expelled. What he thinks about contested questions outside of his vocation within the party cannot be our concern. We can’t be concerned with thought crime. What the architecture of the new order produces will become moral, what the architecture destroys will become immoral. The productive order will be served because the architecture will reward productive contribution at every altitude and structurally exclude the parasitic, not because we will impose a creed or doctrine of “productive virtue” on members who do not yet inhabit the institutions that will make productive virtue real.

¶This is what allows us to be a party of genuinely distinct individuals with different views on contested questions outside of the institutional vision. The atheist and the Christian. The traditionalist and the progressive. All can serve together because constructing the architecture itself is the moral work. The work of building what will produce the moral order of the future is the most morally serious work that any of us can do. There is no higher moral work available to a man in our present situation than building the institution that will replace the Regime. Anyone who has decided to take this commitment upon himself has chosen the most consequential moral action of the present age.

¶This is why we seek power openly. The dissident movements have moralized the pursuit of authority, performed reluctance to wield it, and preferred the position of self-righteous outsider to the responsibilities of governance. We refuse this. Power is the precondition for building the new order. To refuse power is to refuse the work. To refuse power is moral cowardice.

¶Their concern is how their contemporaries judge them. We measure ourselves by how history will judge us.

¶There will be no second masters. We refuse to cooperate with either party. No endorsements, no coalitions, no sponsorships, nothing. We will not compromise. Any member among us who cooperates with the Regime will be exiled. Our absorption will be mechanically impossible. We are the duopoly’s greatest fear: that someone refuses to bargain entirely. The Popular Party will not be bought. We are not an election brand to be rented out to ambitious men. We are a movement larger than any one man’s ambition.

¶The Popular Party stands for the people alone. We do not represent a geography or a demographic. We represent the productive order: those who build, those who serve, those who design, those who create. We will be opposed by those who profit from the Regime’s dysfunction at the public’s expense, and rightly so. We will end economic parasitism. Our platform is Functional Representation. The people deserve to be represented for what they do, not just where they live. Decisions will belong to those who pay the price for those decisions. The Popular Party represents the people who act.

¶The Popular Party will not be dragged into irrelevant culture war bullshit. This is not a subculture. We will not be divided by superficial performances in ideological purity. We will not be divided by race, class, sexuality. We are united together through action. We are not an idea. We are an organization. We are here to work. The Popular Party has visible leadership, clear structure, and explicit aims. Our Party Director is named, accountable, and carries ultimate responsibility for the party’s actions. Wielded with intention, superior maneuver becomes our weapon against the duopoly. We cannot beat them in size, money, or messaging, but we can beat them in leadership, speed, and organization. The Regime is sluggish. They are institutionally incapable of out-deciding. We will win through superior decision-cycle speed. The Regime cannot survive its own mistakes, it cannot adapt. We can.

¶The Popular Party is not going to run candidates for federal offices without first building the infrastructure that will make these victories meaningful. We build from the bottom. School boards, city councils, county offices. We are building a network, a support base, public trust, and a record of competence. We do not contest big offices as some flaccid protest. We contest small offices to build a foundation, and with that foundation, we win.

¶The new political class does not appear out of nowhere. It does not wait until power is taken. It begins now in the party. We hold ourselves to the standards we will enforce. When a man joins, he is submitting to the order he is building. Membership is service, not an endorsement. To be a Popular is to be a servant of the public good. To be a loyal soldier in the struggle against the Regime.

¶The Regime is terminal. Anyone with eyes perceptive enough can see it, though who can know on what timeline. There is no third option, only this, or slow decay. Whether fate brings us glory, infamy, or obscurity, we face tomorrow.
